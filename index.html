Scholarly Impact

Research Program & Funded Projects

My research examines the systemic translation of artificial intelligence and emerging technologies into higher education, focusing on educator readiness, learner metacognition, and scalable pedagogical simulations.

1. Institutional Readiness & Faculty Support

Investigating the structural imbalance between institutional compliance policies and pedagogical enablement. My cross-institutional work highlights that while universities provide ethical mandates, instructors require practice-based, design-centered development to integrate AI meaningfully.

  • Empirical validation using AI-TPACK across STEM and humanities disciplines.
  • IRB-approved TAM2 surveys evaluating perceived usefulness and professional readiness.

2. AI-Simulated Practice Environments

Designing, testing, and deploying multi-agent LLM simulations that allow pre-service educators and professional learners to rehearse complex pedagogical maneuvers within low-risk, high-fidelity settings.

  • ArguMath: Multi-agent platform for orchestrating collective argumentation.
  • Interdisciplinary translation across veterinary clinical data analysis and STEM games.

3. Metacognition & Self-Regulation

Investigating how human-AI co-creation environments impact student metacognition, task persistence, and autonomous problem-solving within enterprise LMS ecosystems.

  • Evaluating Google Gemini integration within Canvas for self-regulation outcomes.
  • Designing actionable learning analytics dashboards to support timely interventions.

Pedagogical Practice

Teaching Philosophy & Course Design

Preparing students and future professionals to navigate workforce automation with adaptable judgment, ethical stewardship, and design rigor.

Philosophy of Teaching

Artificial intelligence is not merely a cognitive crutch or tool; it fundamentally transforms professional practice. My teaching is grounded in backward design, studio critique, and iterative prototyping. I structure learning experiences that require students to:

  • Select tools deliberately: Determine where AI augments human inquiry and where human oversight is vital.
  • Audit outputs critically: Interrogate algorithmic bias, privacy vulnerabilities, and digital accessibility compliance (UDL).
  • Demonstrate applied transfer: Solve authentic, consequential client problems through evidence-based revision cycles.

"The ultimate objective is transfer: learners should graduate capable of applying adaptable professional judgment within unfamiliar, high-stakes environments."
