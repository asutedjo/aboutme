Teaching Philosophy & Graduate Instruction

Select any tab below (or use the Left and Right arrow keys) to explore each dimension of my Teaching Philosophy and the graduate courses I have taught.

Future Readiness

My teaching is guided by a central question: how can education prepare learners to respond thoughtfully and effectively as emerging technologies reshape work? Artificial intelligence is not merely a new instructional tool; it is a changing force in the workforce, altering tasks, professional roles, and the knowledge and judgment people need to remain effective.

I design learner-centered experiences that connect technological understanding with authentic application, critical evaluation, and reflection. Across diverse academic and professional contexts, this approach helps learners build the adaptability, ethical judgment, and practical competence required across career transitions and evolving professional landscapes.

Responsible Practice

I teach AI within the broader context of emerging technologies and changing workforce needs. Learners from varied educational, professional, and technical backgrounds examine how generative and predictive systems work, where they create value, and where human expertise remains essential.

Through authentic disciplinary and workplace problems, learners practice selecting appropriate tools, testing outputs against evidence, and communicating limitations. Attention to bias, privacy, transparency, accessibility, academic integrity, and human oversight positions responsible use as a core dimension of professional readiness rather than an add-on.

Authentic Learning

My graduate teaching in Graphic Communication and Interface Design (LDTC 621) and Designing for Mobile Learning (LDTC 642) has strengthened an applied, project-based approach suited to graduate and lifelong education. I organize learning through backward design, iterative prototyping, peer critique, and evidence-based revision.

Learners move from defining a consequential problem to developing, testing, and explaining a solution. Flexible pathways, formative feedback, and structured practice recognize learners' prior knowledge and competing responsibilities while maintaining rigorous, observable outcomes. The goal is transfer: learners should be able to apply new capabilities confidently in unfamiliar professional settings.

Collaborative Practice

Interdisciplinary collaboration ensures that technology learning remains grounded in professional practice. In ArguMath, I work across education, mathematics education, and computer science to develop an AI-simulated environment in which preservice teachers rehearse complex instructional decisions. In veterinary medicine, I collaborate on clinical data extraction, AI tutoring, and AI literacy outcomes; related faculty partnerships extend this work to political science and leadership education. These experiences have taught me to listen to domain experts, translate emerging needs into teachable competencies, and preserve disciplinary standards.

At the Center for Teaching Excellence, I have designed workshops, webinars, consultations, and hands-on AI learning experiences for faculty. Programs on prompt engineering, higher-order thinking with Copilot, student-centered course design, AI ethics, and course-specific chatbots begin with relevant tasks and produce immediately usable outcomes. I translate this experience into courses, microcredentials, certificates, and professional programs that respond to evolving workforce demands—enabling learners to acquire targeted capabilities, demonstrate readiness, and continue learning as technologies and roles change.

Mentoring for Growth

Teaching and research are mutually reinforcing in my practice. My studies of institutional support, educator readiness, learner adoption, self-regulation, and AI-enabled learning environments provide direct evidence for improving instruction and program design.

I mentor learners to connect coursework with research, professional practice, and community needs while recognizing that readiness involves more than technical proficiency. My goal is to help learners become adaptable citizen-professionals who can use emerging technologies with sound judgment, communicate across disciplines, and consider the consequences of their decisions for organizations and society.
