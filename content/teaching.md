# Teaching Philosophy

My teaching is guided by a central question: how can education prepare learners to respond thoughtfully and effectively as emerging technologies reshape work? Artificial intelligence is not merely a new instructional tool; it is a changing force in the workforce, altering tasks, professional roles, and the knowledge and judgment people need to remain effective. I design learner-centered experiences that connect technological understanding with authentic application, critical evaluation, and reflection.

## Teaching AI in Context & Professional Readiness

I teach AI within the broader context of emerging technologies and changing workforce needs. Learners from varied educational, professional, and technical backgrounds examine how generative and predictive systems work, where they create value, and where human expertise remains essential. Through authentic disciplinary and workplace problems, they practice selecting appropriate tools, testing outputs against evidence, and communicating limitations. Attention to bias, privacy, transparency, accessibility, academic integrity, and human oversight positions responsible use as a core dimension of professional readiness rather than an add-on.

## Studio Pedagogy & Iterative Prototyping

My graduate teaching in Graphic Communication and Interface Design (LDTC 621) and Designing for Mobile Learning (LDTC 642) has strengthened an applied, project-based approach. I organize learning through backward design, iterative prototyping, peer critique, and evidence-based revision. Learners move from defining a consequential problem to developing, testing, and explaining a solution. Flexible pathways, formative feedback, and structured practice recognize learners' prior knowledge while maintaining rigorous, observable outcomes. The goal is transfer: learners should be able to apply new capabilities confidently in unfamiliar professional settings.

## Interdisciplinary Collaboration & Lifelong Learning

Interdisciplinary collaboration ensures that technology learning remains grounded in professional practice. Across education, mathematics education, computer science, and veterinary medicine, my partnerships have taught me to listen to domain experts, translate emerging needs into teachable competencies, and preserve disciplinary standards. Teaching and research are mutually reinforcing in my practice, helping learners become adaptable citizen-professionals who use emerging technologies with sound judgment and consider the consequences of their decisions for organizations and society.
