# Research Statement & Agenda

## Research Statement
My research develops and evaluates evidence-based approaches for integrating emerging technologies, particularly Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), into higher education. I examine how these technologies can improve teaching, learning, and faculty practice while remaining practical, responsible, and responsive to institutional contexts.

## Research Agenda
My research is guided by a central question: How can emerging technologies, particularly GenAI, be translated into teaching practices that measurably enhance learning while supporting responsible adoption by educators and institutions? I address this question across three interconnected areas: institutional support and readiness, educator and learner adoption, and the design and evaluation of student-centered learning experiences.

## Readiness and Adoption
At the institutional level, my paper “Generative AI in Higher Education: A Cross-Institutional Study on Faculty Preparation and Resources,” published in Studies in Technology Enhanced Learning, found that research-intensive universities provided strong policy and ethical guidance but limited interactive, research-oriented, and practice-based support for faculty. This imbalance shows how institutional approaches can prioritize compliance over instructional experimentation, establishing the need to better understand faculty readiness and support.

Building on these findings, my study “Assessing Faculty Self-Perceived Knowledge in Using Generative AI to Teach 21st-Century Skills,” published in Frontiers in Education, found that faculty possessed strong pedagogical and content knowledge but lacked confidence in integrating GenAI with pedagogy. The study identifies a specific readiness gap and supports hands-on, design-centered faculty development rather than information-only training.

I extend this research from faculty readiness to student adoption through IRB-approved survey research using the Technology Acceptance Model 2 (TAM2). By examining perceived usefulness, ease of use, relevance, and professional readiness, this work provides a learner-centered perspective that complements the faculty findings and informs course design, institutional policy, and instructional decision-making.

## Learning Interventions
A central strand of my research translates findings about readiness and adoption into the design and evaluation of learning interventions. As Co-Investigator on “Generative AI Integration in Veterinary Education: AI Data Entry, AI Tutor, and AI Literacy Outcomes,” I oversee the evaluation of GenAI’s effects on efficiency, accuracy, and AI literacy in veterinary education. The study’s first paper, “ChatGPT versus Veterinary Students for Clinical Data Extraction: A Mixed-Methods Study,” is under review. Together, this work examines evidence-based and ethical models of GenAI integration in a high-stakes professional context.

I extend this intervention-focused research into teacher preparation through ArguMath, an AI-simulated environment in which preservice teachers rehearse complex instructional practices through multi-agent, large-language-model simulations. Our study, “ArguMath: AI-Simulated Environment for Pre-service Teacher Preparation on Facilitating Collective Mathematical Argumentation,” was presented at the 2026 International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Education in Seoul, South Korea. Using design-based research, the project integrates learning theory, human-computer interaction, and empirical evaluation to investigate scalable, low-risk opportunities for pedagogical practice and reflection.

I further extend this learner-centered agenda from perceptions to measurable learning processes by collaborating with two faculty fellows to investigate how a learning environment integrating Google Gemini with Canvas affects students’ self-regulation and metacognition. This work examines how GenAI influences learning behaviors and provides evidence for designing AI-supported learning environments that promote more intentional and self-regulated learning.

## Research Trajectory
Building on these interconnected strands, my research trajectory advances three priorities: developing participatory faculty-development models that strengthen pedagogical uses of GenAI; expanding AI-mediated simulations and tutors that support complex teaching and learning practices; and establishing evidence-based frameworks that connect institutional policy, faculty support, and instructional innovation. Across these priorities, I aim to produce theoretically grounded, empirically rigorous, and practically meaningful scholarship that helps educators, learners, and institutions navigate the evolving landscape of technology-enhanced education.
