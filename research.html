Research Statement

My research develops and evaluates evidence-based approaches for integrating emerging technologies, particularly Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), into higher education. I examine how these technologies can improve teaching, learning, and faculty practice while remaining practical, responsible, and responsive to institutional contexts.

Central Research Question

How can emerging technologies, particularly GenAI, be translated into teaching practices that measurably enhance learning while supporting responsible adoption by educators and institutions? I address this question through three interconnected areas: institutional support structures, faculty development and teaching practice, and the design of student-centered learning experiences with measurable outcomes.

Institutional Support & Faculty Readiness

At the institutional level, my paper "Generative AI in Higher Education: A Cross-Institutional Study on Faculty Preparation and Resources," published in Studies in Technology Enhanced Learning, found that research-intensive universities provided strong policy and ethical guidance but limited interactive, research-oriented, and practice-based support for faculty. This imbalance shows how institutional approaches can prioritize compliance over instructional experimentation.

Building on these institutional findings, my study "Assessing Faculty Self-Perceived Knowledge in Using Generative AI to Teach 21st-Century Skills," published in Frontiers in Education, found that faculty possessed strong pedagogical and content knowledge but lacked confidence in integrating GenAI with pedagogy. Extending this analysis from faculty readiness to student adoption, I use the Technology Acceptance Model 2 (TAM2) in IRB-approved survey research to examine how students perceive the value of AI use across usefulness, relevance, and professional readiness.

Classroom Interventions & AI-Simulated Environments

A central strand of my research translates these findings into classroom-based interventions. As Co-Investigator on "Generative AI Integration in Veterinary Education: AI Data Entry, AI Tutor, and AI Literacy Outcomes," I oversee the evaluation of GenAI's effects on efficiency, accuracy, and AI literacy in a high-stakes clinical context.

Extending this intervention-focused work into teacher preparation, I collaborate on ArguMath, an AI-simulated classroom environment in which preservice teachers rehearse complex instructional practices through multi-agent, large-language-model simulations. Presented at the 2026 International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIED) in Seoul, South Korea, the project combines learning theory, human-computer interaction, and empirical evaluation to develop scalable, low-risk simulations for pedagogical practice.

To extend my learner-centered agenda from perceptions to measurable learning processes, I lead a funded study investigating how a learning environment integrating Google Gemini with Canvas affects students' self-regulation and metacognition.

Future Research Directions

Looking forward, my research agenda will advance three connected priorities: developing participatory faculty-development models that strengthen pedagogical uses of GenAI; expanding AI-mediated simulations and tutors that support complex teaching and learning practices; and establishing evidence-based frameworks that align institutional policy, faculty support, and instructional innovation.
